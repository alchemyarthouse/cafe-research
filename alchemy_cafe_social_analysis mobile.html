Education as Entertainment

What they post
The "DIY or Buy" Series. Radical Cost Transparency.
Manager Serena Walker hosts a recurring video series that breaks down whether a drink is worth buying or making at home — including the exact ingredient cost. The Raspberry Danish Latte: $2.46 to make at home. $8 in-store. That level of honesty should scare a business owner. Instead it built massive trust. "When you really break it down, you see how little profit we're actually making." That vulnerability creates the emotional connection that turns viewers into evangelists. Drinks are dessert-inspired: sticky toffee pudding latte, dark and stormy matcha.

The Independent Café
Social Playbook
Six cross-cutting lessons from three wildly different brands — each of which found a completely different path to the same result: deep, loyal, commercially meaningful communities.
Applied Research
01
Give Away the Kitchen
Little Joy and Onyx both share how they make things. One gives away entire recipes to competitors. The other publishes championship routines and exact brew ratios. The counterintuitive logic: transparency creates desire. Showing people how something is made doesn't reduce the magic — it deepens it. No one actually wants to do the work. The recipe becomes a beacon. The skill becomes the justification for the price.
Onyx · Little Joy
02
Make the Person the Face. Not the Brand.
Serena Walker at Little Joy. Morgan Eckroth at Onyx. Neither is a "brand ambassador" — both are real people doing real jobs on camera. Audiences follow people, not logos. When a human face carries the story, the parasocial connection runs deeper. Morgan's audience followed her World Championship journey like a sports season. That's earned loyalty no ad buy replicates.
Onyx · Little Joy
03
Engineer the Shareable Object
Blank Street's matcha green cup is the shareable object. Little Joy's raspberry Danish latte is the shareable object. Every drink, every seasonal special should be designed to make someone want to photograph it. The product is the media. 62% of consumers check a brand's social before visiting. The drink your customer photographed last week is your best ad.
Blank Street · Little Joy
04
Hyperlocal × Global Is Not a Contradiction
Blank Street activates local creators for each neighbourhood opening. Little Joy's global recipe map pins every city where the drink lives. Both show that the most powerful social strategy starts with a specific, real community — and radiates outward. Being deeply local gives you authenticity. The internet handles global amplification. Trying to be global first is the mistake.
Blank Street · Little Joy
05
Build Owned Channels Alongside Rented Ones
Blank Street's Regulars membership is the clearest example: 5,000 paying members, 4,000 on a waitlist, visiting 6× per week. That's an owned audience — not subject to algorithm changes or platform shifts. Onyx's email list builds the same muscle. Social media is the top of funnel. The owned channel is the asset. Rented reach is borrowed. Owned reach compounds.
Blank Street · Onyx
06
IRL Is Still the Best Content Studio
All three brands prove the best content comes from physical reality: a café floor, a competition stage, a coffee cart in Brooklyn. Blank Street stages celebrity moments in actual stores. Morgan films at the bar. Serena films during service. The camera is not separate from the work — it's embedded in it. The most shareable content for a café will almost always come from inside the four walls.
